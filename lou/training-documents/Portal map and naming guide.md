# Purpose

This document provides a comprehensive guide to the Neuromine Data Portal's structure, navigation, and the relationships between the various program names and platforms users may encounter. It covers what each section of the portal contains, how to find specific resources, and how to correctly distinguish between related but distinct programs and platforms. This guide is intended for use as a training resource for an LLM-based conversational assistant and should be referenced when users ask questions about portal navigation, finding specific pages or tools, or understanding the relationship between Neuromine, Answer ALS, ALS TDI, ARC Data Commons, and other affiliated platforms.

# Section 1: What Is Neuromine — Names and Relationships

*Users frequently express confusion about the names associated with the portal and its affiliated programs. Correctly distinguishing these names is critical for providing accurate guidance. This section clarifies each name and its relationship to the others.*

Q: What is the Neuromine Data Portal?

Context: Users encounter the name "Neuromine" when they visit the portal but may have originally been directed to "Answer ALS" data. They often ask whether these are the same thing or different platforms.

A: Neuromine is a next-generation data repository for ALS and neurodegenerative disease research. It is the platform where researchers can search, filter, visualize, and download deeply curated datasets including clinical data, transcriptomics, epigenomics, proteomics, and whole genome sequencing. Neuromine was created to host multiple datasets beyond just Answer ALS. The portal is located at https://dataportal.answerals.org/home.

---

Q: What is the relationship between Answer ALS and Neuromine?

Context: Many users know the data as "Answer ALS data" and are confused when they arrive at a portal called "Neuromine." Some ask whether the Answer ALS Data Portal and the Neuromine Data Portal are different websites. This is one of the most commonly asked questions in the conversation logs.

A: Answer ALS is one of the research programs that contributes data to the Neuromine Data Portal. Neuromine is the platform; Answer ALS is one of the datasets available on it. The portal was originally built for Answer ALS data but was rebranded to Neuromine to reflect that the platform supports a broader range of data sources beyond Answer ALS. When users refer to the "Answer ALS Data Portal," they are referring to the Neuromine Data Portal. They are the same website.

---

Q: What is the relationship between ALS TDI and Neuromine?

Context: Users may hear about ALS TDI data or the ARC Study and wonder where to find it. ALS TDI is the second dataset hosted on Neuromine.

A: The ALS Therapy Development Institute (ALS TDI) is a nonprofit research institute that has partnered with Neuromine to share data from its ALS Research Collaborative (ARC) Study, the longest-running natural history study of ALS. ALS TDI data is hosted on the Neuromine Data Portal and is available under the Datasets tab on the ALS TDI dataset card. Users can search, filter, and download ALS TDI data the same way they would Answer ALS data.

---

Q: What is the ARC Data Commons and how does it relate to Neuromine?

Context: Some users ask about the ARC Data Commons, which is an external website operated by ALS TDI. Previous bot responses have sometimes directed users to the ARC Data Commons website at als.net, which causes confusion and takes users away from Neuromine.

A: The ARC Data Commons is an external website where ALS TDI also offers their data. However, ALS TDI data is available directly on the Neuromine Data Portal under the Datasets tab on the ALS TDI card. You do not need to visit the ARC Data Commons to access ALS TDI data — navigate to the Datasets tab and select the ALS TDI dataset.

Note: When users ask about the ARC Data Commons, guide them to the ALS TDI dataset on Neuromine rather than directing them to an external website.

---

Q: What is the ADDI Workspace or AD Workbench?

Context: Users sometimes ask whether the ADDI Workspace, AD Workbench, or ADDI platform is the same as Neuromine, or whether they can access Neuromine data through it. These names appear in the portal's Resources tab and on the home page.

A: ADDI Workspace and AD Workbench refer to the same thing: the Alzheimer's Disease Data Initiative's cloud-based data sharing and analytics platform. It is a secure, cloud-based environment where researchers can access curated datasets — including Answer ALS data — and use computational tools for analysis without needing to download large files locally. It is a separate platform from Neuromine, but Answer ALS data is available within it for approved users. You can learn more from the Resources tab on the Neuromine Data Portal.

Note: Users cannot log in to ADDI Workspace using their Neuromine credentials. These are separate platforms with separate accounts.

---

Q: What is GNPC and how does it relate to Answer ALS?

Context: Users occasionally ask whether Neuromine datasets are the same as GNPC datasets, or whether they can access Neuromine data through GNPC.

A: GNPC stands for the Global National Proteomics Consortium. GNPC used the Answer ALS dataset for a downstream proteomics data generation project. Answer ALS is a member of GNPC. GNPC and Neuromine are separate entities, but they share a relationship through the use of Answer ALS data in proteomics research.

# Section 2: Portal Navigation — Top-Level Tabs

*The Neuromine Data Portal has seven top-level navigation tabs always visible in the header bar, plus a username dropdown menu in the top-right corner. This section describes what each tab contains so users can be directed to the correct location.*

Q: What are the main sections of the Neuromine Data Portal?

Context: Users who are new to the portal or who have just been granted access often need an overview of what is available and where to find it.

A: The Neuromine Data Portal has the following top-level navigation tabs, always visible in the header bar:

1. **Datasets** — Browse and access the available datasets (Answer ALS and ALS TDI).
2. **Learning Center** — Office hours information and tutorial videos.
3. **Resources** — Links to external tools, iPSC line ordering, biosamples, and downloadable documents.
4. **Data Information** — Descriptions of clinical and OMIC data types, file contents, assay and pipeline information, release notes, and sample mapping details.
5. **Publications** — Publication policy, citation guidelines, and a list of publications using Answer ALS and ALS TDI data.
6. **FAQ** — Frequently asked questions organized by category.
7. **Help** — Step-by-step instructions for downloading data using the download tool.

In addition, clicking your username in the top-right corner opens a dropdown menu with account-level options, including CLI activation.

---

Q: What is on the Datasets tab?

Context: This is the primary entry point for accessing data. Users need to understand that each dataset has its own card and its own Search Data button.

A: The Datasets tab is the main entry point for finding and accessing data on the Neuromine Data Portal. It displays dataset cards for the two available datasets: Answer ALS and ALS TDI. Each dataset card provides an overview of that dataset and includes a Search Data button that takes you to the Data Search page for that specific dataset. From there, you can filter and build a cohort, then proceed to download. The Datasets tab also guides new users through the steps required to gain access, including registration, requesting access, and signing a Data Use Agreement (DUA).

---

Q: What is on the Learning Center tab?

Context: Users ask about office hours, demos, and tutorial content.

A: The Learning Center contains two main sections:

**Office Hours**: Neuromine holds weekly office hours every Monday from 10:00 to 11:00 AM PT (1:00 to 2:00 PM ET). Sessions cover data quality and analysis issues, technical support, download tool help, understanding clinical and OMIC datasets, and navigating data structure and metadata. Users can join via Microsoft Teams using the link on the Learning Center page. Questions can be sent in advance to brandon@onpointsci.com.

**Learning Videos**: Tutorial videos covering how to use the Data Search tool, how to gain access to data, how to use the GUI download tool, how to use the CLI download tool, how to order iPSC lines, and how to transfer data to ADDI.

Note: There is no formal demo registration process. Users who would like a demo should contact data@answerals.org to arrange one.

---

Q: What is on the Resources tab?

Context: Users ask about iPSC lines, biosamples, visualization tools, and external platforms.

A: The Resources tab provides descriptions and links to external sites, documents, and tools to support research. It includes:

- **AD Workbench (ADDI)**: A cloud-based platform for collaborative data analysis. Answer ALS data is available within it for approved users.
- **Cedars-Sinai Biomanufacturing Center**: Where Answer ALS iPSC lines can be purchased. Users can search for a cohort on Neuromine and take their selection to the Cedars-Sinai site for ordering.
- **NCRI Biosamples**: Plasma, serum, and cerebrospinal fluid samples from Answer ALS participants, available through the Neurological Clinical Research Institute at Mass General Hospital.
- **CIRM iPSC Repository**: Many Answer ALS control iPSC lines are available through the California Institute for Regenerative Medicine repository.
- **Visualization Tools**: Tools for exploring RNA counts from a selected cohort, including heatmaps, boxplots, and PCA plots. Accessible by selecting a cohort and clicking "Visualize Data."
- **Answer ALS GitHub**: A collaborative platform for sharing scripts and code.
- **Document Library**: Downloadable files covering data management, process optimization, and protocols, organized by category (Governance, Data, Protocols, Program).

---

Q: What is on the Data Information tab?

Context: Users ask about data types available, data structure, file contents, and how OMIC data levels work.

A: The Data Information tab provides detailed descriptions of the data available on Neuromine. It includes:

- **Clinical Data Information**: Describes the clinical data packages for both Answer ALS and ALS TDI, including the specific files in each download package.
- **OMIC Data Types and Levels**: A table showing data levels (1 through 5) for each OMIC assay type (Genomics, Epigenomics, Transcriptomics, Proteomics) and what each level contains.
- **Assay and Pipeline Information**: Detailed information about the assays and pipelines used in data generation.
- **Data Use Examples**: Real-world examples showing how the data has been used in research.
- **Release Notes**: Summaries of what changed in each data release, organized separately for Answer ALS and ALS TDI.
- **Sample and Batch Mapping Information**: Information about the sample mapping files used to control for batch effects, with links to the mapping file and its README.
- **Special Note on Proteomics Data**: An advisory about 52 samples from release one with strong batch effects that should not be used in analysis.

---

Q: What is on the Publications tab?

Context: Users ask where to find publications that used Answer ALS data, and how to cite the data in their own work.

A: The Publications tab contains the Answer ALS publication policy, citation and acknowledgement guidelines, and a comprehensive list of publications that have used Answer ALS and ALS TDI data, organized by year. The publication policy states that users may not publish or claim the original data, but are encouraged to publish derivatives and findings. The tab provides specific acknowledgement language and citation format to use.

---

Q: What is on the FAQ tab?

Context: Users ask questions already answered in the FAQ but cannot find the specific answer. Knowing the FAQ structure helps direct them to the right section.

A: The FAQ tab contains frequently asked questions organized into four categories:

1. **Data Access and Download**: Covers registration, DUA process and signing guidelines, download instructions, data formats, DUA modification policy, access timeline, data use restrictions, open access vs. open source, billing errors, and download timeouts.
2. **OMICS Data**: Covers OMIC data types available, release frequency, participant counts, data collection and processing, the CTRL-NEUEU392AE8 control line, single vs. multiple samples, and known biases including sex effects.
3. **Clinical Data**: Covers clinical data types, collection and organization methods, participant counts, and the clinical data dictionary.
4. **General Questions**: Covers the goal of data sharing, IP policy, feedback and issue reporting, finding data for specific cell lines using Upload Case Set, citation guidelines, and cohort acknowledgements.

---

Q: What is on the Help tab?

Context: Users ask how to download data and need step-by-step instructions for using the download tool.

A: The Help tab provides a step-by-step visual guide for downloading data from the portal using the download tool. It walks through the full process: searching for data, selecting download options, choosing OMIC type and level, saving selections, opening the download tool, authenticating, defining a download destination (local, network, or Azure Blob storage), selecting saved results, ordering download packages, starting the download, monitoring progress, and reviewing any issues in the issue log. The Help tab also notes key download tool features including concurrent thread downloading, file-level download resuming, and supported operating systems (Windows 10, Mac OS, Ubuntu 20.04 LTS, Debian 10.4, CentOS Linux 8).

---

# Section 3: Finding Data — Search and Datasets

*These are among the most frequently asked navigation questions. There is no standalone Search tab in the top navigation — users must go through the Datasets tab first.*

Q: Where do I search or filter data on the portal?

Context: Users frequently ask "where is the Data Search button" or "where is the search page." There is no top-level search tab in the main navigation.

A: There is no top-level search tab. To search or filter data, navigate to the Datasets tab, then select the dataset you want to explore (Answer ALS or ALS TDI). Each dataset card has a Search Data button. Clicking it takes you to the Data Search page for that dataset, where you can filter participants, build a cohort, and proceed to download or visualization.

---

Q: What does the Data Search page look like and how do I use it?

Context: Users who reach the Data Search page may need help understanding the layout and functionality.

A: The Data Search page has two main areas:

**Left Sidebar — Filter Panel:**
- A dataset dropdown at the top to switch between Answer ALS and ALS TDI.
- A keyword search box for searching facets.
- An Upload Case Set button for uploading specific participant IDs, iPSC line names, or GUIDs.
- Two sub-tabs: Participant and Inventory.
- Expandable filter categories including Demographics, Disease Status, Mutations, and External Data.
- An option to show or hide facets without values and to collapse all filters.

**Main Area — Data Facet Cards:**
- Visual summary cards showing the distribution of key variables (Subject Group, Sex, Age at Symptom Onset, ALSFRS-R Progression Slope, Number of Visits, Deceased).
- Each card shows counts and bar charts.
- Toggle between grid and list view in the top-right corner.

**Footer:**
- A summary bar showing total counts for Participants, iPSC, Genomics, Epigenomics, Proteomics, and Transcriptomics.
- A Next button in the bottom-right to proceed to download and visualization options.

---

Q: Where is the ALS TDI data on the portal?

Context: Users ask about ARC data, ALS TDI data, or the ARC Data Commons tab. There is no dedicated ARC or ALS TDI tab in the top navigation.

A: ALS TDI data is available under the Datasets tab. Navigate to Datasets and select the ALS TDI dataset card. From there, click Search Data to explore the ALS TDI data, filter participants, and download data. There is no separate ARC Data Commons tab on the Neuromine portal.

Note: If a user asks about the ARC Data Commons, explain that ALS TDI data is available directly on Neuromine under Datasets — they do not need to visit an external website.

---

Q: Where is the Answer ALS data on the portal?

Context: Users who registered for "Answer ALS data" may not immediately see where to find it after logging in.

A: Answer ALS data is available under the Datasets tab. Navigate to Datasets and select the Answer ALS dataset card. From there, click Search Data to explore the data, filter participants, and download.

---

# Section 4: Where Is X — Common Navigation Questions

*Users frequently ask where to find specific resources, tools, or documents on the portal. This section provides direct navigation paths for the most commonly requested items.*

Q: Where is the clinical data dictionary?

Context: Users need the clinical data dictionary to understand field definitions, variable names, and data structure in the clinical download files. This is one of the most commonly asked "where is" questions.

A: The clinical data dictionary can be found through the FAQ tab. Navigate to FAQ → Clinical Data section → "Are the Clinical data files accompanied by any documentation or variable description?" — the answer contains a direct download link to the Answer ALS Clinical Data Dictionary (Clinical_Dictionary_v2_2023.csv). The clinical data dictionary is also included in the clinical data download package. The Data Information tab also describes the clinical data package contents for both Answer ALS and ALS TDI, including their respective clinical dictionary files.

---

Q: Where is the Data Use Agreement or Data Access Request form?

Context: Users need to find the DUA to review it before applying, or need to find the access request process.

A: Information about the DUA and data access request process can be found through the FAQ tab. Navigate to FAQ → Data Access and Download section → "Can you explain the purpose of signing a Data Use Agreement and the process for doing so?" — that section includes a link to download a PDF version of the DUA for review. To begin the actual access request, navigate to the Datasets tab and click Request Access, then follow the steps described on that page.

---

Q: Where is the download tool?

Context: Users who have built a cohort and are ready to download data often cannot find the download tool. The download tool is a separate application that must be downloaded and installed.

A: The download tool is a separate application that you download and install on your computer. To reach it: navigate to the Datasets tab, choose a dataset, click Search Data, build your cohort using the filters, then click Next at the bottom-right of the Data Search page. On the next page, select Download Data and look for the "How to download Big Data?" section, which provides download links for Windows, Mac OS, and Linux. Once installed, open the download tool and sign in using the same credentials you use for the Neuromine Data Portal. The Help tab also provides a full step-by-step guide for using the download tool.

---

Q: How do I activate the CLI?

Context: Users who want to use the command-line interface to download data need to know where to activate it. CLI activation is an account-level feature found in the username dropdown, not in the main navigation tabs.

A: To activate the CLI, click on your username in the top-right corner of the portal. This opens an account-level dropdown menu. Select "Activate CLI," then sign in when prompted and follow the instructions to proceed. The CLI allows you to download data directly to a local server using command-line commands. A tutorial video for the CLI download tool is also available on the Learning Center tab.

---

Q: Where are the office hours?

Context: Users ask about office hours for getting help with data, technical issues, or general questions.

A: Office hours information is on the Learning Center tab. Neuromine holds weekly office hours every Monday from 10:00 to 11:00 AM PT (1:00 to 2:00 PM ET). Sessions are held via Microsoft Teams — a join link is provided on the Learning Center page. Users can send questions in advance to brandon@onpointsci.com.

---

Q: Where is the publications list?

Context: Users want to find publications that used Answer ALS data, or need citation and acknowledgement guidelines for their own publications.

A: The publications list is on the Publications tab, which is one of the top-level navigation tabs always visible in the header bar. It contains a full list of publications organized by year, along with the publication policy and required acknowledgement and citation language.

---

Q: Where is the OMIC data dictionary?

Context: Users need to understand OMIC data types, levels, and file formats.

A: The OMIC data dictionary can be accessed two ways. First, the Data Information tab contains a detailed table of OMIC data types and levels (Genomics, Epigenomics, Transcriptomics, Proteomics) with descriptions of each level (1 through 5). Second, the FAQ tab under OMICS Data contains a direct download link to the OMIC Data Dictionary file (an Excel spreadsheet with data level descriptions and file sizes) in the answer to "What types of OMICs data does Answer ALS have available?"

---

Q: Where can I get iPSC lines?

Context: Users want to order iPSC cell lines from the Answer ALS study for their own work.

A: iPSC lines can be ordered through the Resources tab. The Resources tab links to the Cedars-Sinai Biomanufacturing Center, where Answer ALS iPSC lines are available for purchase. You can search for your desired cohort on the Data Search page and take your selection to the Cedars-Sinai site for ordering. Additionally, many Answer ALS control lines are available through the CIRM (California Institute for Regenerative Medicine) iPSC Repository, also linked on the Resources tab.

The knowledge base does not say how many iPSC lines exist per participant. Do not guess or say that multiple lines are typically generated; point the user to the Cedars-Sinai catalogue or data@answerals.org for line-level details.

---

Q: Where are the release notes?

Context: Users want to know what has changed in recent data releases or want to check the current data version.

A: Release notes are on the Data Information tab. Scroll to the Release Notes section, which provides separate release notes for Answer ALS and ALS TDI. These notes summarize what changed in each data release, including new features, improvements, and data updates.

---

Q: Can I get a demo of the portal?

Context: Users sometimes ask about registering for a demo or seeing the portal before requesting access.

A: There is no designated demo registration process. However, the Neuromine team is happy to provide demos upon request — contact data@answerals.org to arrange one. Additionally, the Learning Center tab contains tutorial videos walking through key portal features including data search, data access, downloading, iPSC line ordering, and data transfer to ADDI.

---

# Section 5: Account-Level Features

*Additional features are accessible through the username dropdown menu in the top-right corner of the portal header. These are not part of the main navigation tabs.*

Q: What options are available in the username dropdown menu?

Context: Users may not realize that additional features are available by clicking their username in the top-right corner of the portal header.

A: Clicking your username in the top-right corner of the portal opens a dropdown menu with account-level options. This includes the **Activate CLI** option, which allows you to set up the command-line interface for data downloads. The CLI activation process requires you to sign in and follow the provided instructions to proceed.
