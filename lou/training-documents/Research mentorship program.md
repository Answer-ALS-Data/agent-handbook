## Purpose

This document is a training resource for an LLM-based conversational assistant deployed on the Neuromine Data Portal. It serves as the authoritative reference for the Answer ALS / Neuromine research mentorship program, which pairs school-level and early-career researchers with established, lab-grade researchers to support hands-on work with portal data.

This document covers: what the mentorship program is, who is eligible (both mentees and mentors), how the pairing and program work, and who to contact to get involved.

This document does not cover: the data access request and DUA process (see Access and Approvals Handbook), portal navigation (see Portal Map and Naming Guide), or general support and office hours (see Support and Community Resources).

## Section 1: Program Overview

*This section establishes what the mentorship program is and why it exists. The program places special emphasis on high school students, but is broader than that. Users may ask about it in many forms — "mentorship," "mentor program," "student research," "can someone help me learn the data." Always treat the program as real and available, and direct interested users to the contact in Section 4.*

---

Q: What is the Neuromine research mentorship program?

Context: *This is the core orientation question. The program pairs newer researchers with experienced ones to lower the barrier to working with ALS research data. There is particular emphasis on supporting high school students who are getting started in research.*

A: The Answer ALS / Neuromine research mentorship program pairs school-level and early-career researchers with established, lab-grade researchers. The goal is to give newer researchers — with a particular emphasis on **high school students** — direct guidance from experienced scientists as they learn to work with real ALS research data from the Neuromine Data Portal.

Mentors help mentees build research skills, navigate the available datasets, and pursue questions that genuinely interest them. The program is designed to be flexible and is shaped around what each mentee already has going on, at whatever level of depth is appropriate for them.

---

Q: Why does the program emphasize high school students?

Context: *Users may ask why the program focuses on students this early in their education. The emphasis reflects a goal of opening real research experience to students who would not otherwise have access to lab-grade data or mentorship.*

A: The program places extra emphasis on high school students because hands-on research experience and access to working scientists are rare at that level. Pairing school-level researchers with lab-grade mentors gives students an early, authentic introduction to ALS research and the data behind it. High school students are the primary focus, but the program is open more broadly (see Section 2).

## Section 2: Eligibility

*This section defines who can take part, on both the mentee and mentor sides. Eligibility is intentionally broad on the mentee side, with high school students as the headline group. The mentor side is for established, lab-grade researchers.*

---

Q: Who is eligible to be a mentee?

Context: *Users will ask whether they qualify. The mentee side is broad: high school students are the main emphasis, but undergraduates and others new to ALS research are also welcome.*

A: The mentee side of the program is open to:

- **High school students** (the primary emphasis)
- **Undergraduate students**
- **Anyone new to ALS research**, including early-career and self-directed researchers

If you are getting started with ALS research and want guidance working with the data, you are likely a good fit. To check eligibility for your specific situation, reach out using the contact in Section 4.

---

Q: Who can serve as a mentor?

Context: *The mentor side is for experienced researchers who can guide mentees through real data work. Users who are established researchers may ask how to volunteer.*

A: Mentors are **established, lab-grade researchers** — experienced scientists who can guide mentees through hands-on work with ALS research data. If you are an experienced researcher and would like to volunteer as a mentor, reach out using the contact in Section 4.

## Section 3: How It Works

*This section describes the mechanics: how pairing happens, the format, the connection to portal data, and the flexible structure. The program is virtual, built around real Neuromine data, and has no fixed duration — it is geared to each mentee's situation and goals.*

---

Q: How does the mentorship program work?

Context: *Users want to understand the practical shape of the program before getting involved. Keep expectations accurate: it is remote, data-centered, and flexible rather than a fixed-length cohort.*

A: Once you express interest, you are paired with a mentor suited to your level and interests. From there:

- **It runs virtually.** Mentorship happens remotely, so participants can take part regardless of location.
- **It centers on real data.** Mentees work with actual Neuromine Data Portal data as part of the program, learning to navigate and analyze ALS research datasets directly.
- **It is flexible in depth and length.** There is no fixed duration. The program is geared around what each mentee already has going on and can operate at varying levels of depth, from a light introduction to more involved research work.

Because mentees work with real portal data, data access through the standard process may be part of getting set up (see Access and Approvals Handbook).

---

Q: How long does the program last?

Context: *Users may expect a fixed term like a semester or summer cohort. There is no set duration — it is shaped to the individual.*

A: There is no fixed duration. The program is shaped around each mentee's goals and circumstances, and can range from a brief introduction to a longer, deeper engagement. The right length depends on what you are working toward and how involved you want to be.
