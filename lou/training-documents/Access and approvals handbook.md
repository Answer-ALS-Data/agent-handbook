# Purpose

This document provides answers to frequently asked questions about gaining and managing data access on the Neuromine Data Portal. It covers the end-to-end process for submitting a Data Use Agreement (DUA), the roles and eligibility requirements for applicants, how to manage collaborators, the approval timeline, DocuSign signing steps, common access errors, and data sharing rules. This guide is intended for use as a training resource for an LLM-based conversational assistant and should be referenced when users ask questions related to data access requests, DUA status, collaborator management, approval processes, or data sharing restrictions.

# Process Overview

The Neuromine Data Portal requires all users to submit a Data Use Agreement (DUA) before accessing datasets. The DUA process involves multiple parties: the data requester, a Principal Investigator (PI), an Authorized Business Official, any collaborators, and the Neuromine Data Access Committee (DAC). The process includes an initial screening by the Neuromine team, a formal review by the DAC, and a DocuSign signing step that occurs after approval. Understanding this process end to end is the most common area of confusion for new users.

# Section 1: DUA and DAR Process

*Users frequently ask about the steps required to gain data access, the relationship between the DUA and the Data Access Request, and what happens at each stage of the process. Many users are unsure whether they have already signed the DUA or what comes next after submitting their request. This section covers the full process from start to finish.*

**Process rule:** Whenever Lou describes how to get access, it includes the Data Access Committee (DAC) review, and it says the DUA is signed through DocuSign only AFTER the DAC approves. Never tell a user to sign the DUA before or while requesting access.

Q: How do I request access to data on the Neuromine Data Portal?

Context: This is the single most common access-related question. Users arrive at the portal for the first time and need to understand the full process. The steps involve multiple stages and multiple parties, and users often do not realize that DUA signing happens after approval, not during submission.

A: The process for requesting data access involves the following steps:

1. Create an account on the Neuromine Data Portal.
2. Navigate to the Datasets tab.
3. You will be prompted to fill out a Data Use Agreement (DUA) request form. This form asks for your information, your Principal Investigator (PI), your institution's Authorized Business Official, any collaborators who will use the data, and a data use proposal explaining why you need the data.
4. After you submit the form, the Neuromine team reviews your request for completeness. They check for the presence of a PI, a Business Official with contractual signatory authority, an adequate data use proposal, and any listed collaborators.
5. If the request is complete, it is forwarded to the Data Access Committee (DAC) for final review. The DAC is the governing body that controls who receives data access.
6. If the DAC approves your request, DocuSign envelopes are sent to all parties on the DUA for signing.
7. Once all user signatures are received, Neuromine countersigns the DUA and grants data access to your account.

If any information is missing or incomplete, the Neuromine team will reach out to you before forwarding the request to the DAC. For questions at any stage, contact data@answerals.org.

Q: What is a Data Use Agreement (DUA) and why do I need to sign one?

Context: Users sometimes do not understand why a DUA is required or what it governs. The DUA is a legally binding agreement that controls how data is accessed, used, disclosed, and safeguarded.

A: A Data Use Agreement (DUA) is a legally binding contract between you (and your institution) and the Answer ALS Foundation. It governs your access to, use of, disclosure of, and safeguarding of de-identified data from the Neuromine Data Repository. The DUA ensures that all users comply with applicable privacy and data protection laws and that the data is used solely for the scientific research described in your approved request. You must sign the DUA before you can access any datasets on the portal.

Q: What is the relationship between the DUA and the Data Access Request (DAR)?

Context: Users are often confused by the terms DUA and DAR and whether they are the same thing or separate steps. The DUA form submission on the portal functions as the Data Access Request. The DUA document itself is signed later via DocuSign after the DAC approves the request.

A: When you fill out and submit the DUA request form on the Datasets tab, that submission serves as your Data Access Request (DAR). The form captures your information, your PI, your Business Official, your collaborators, and your data use proposal. After the Neuromine team and the Data Access Committee review and approve your request, the actual DUA document is sent to all parties via DocuSign for signing. So the DAR is the request you submit, and the DUA is the agreement you sign after that request is approved.

Q: Did I already sign the DUA when I registered or applied for access?

Context: Users frequently believe they signed the DUA during account registration or when they submitted the request form. This is incorrect. DUA signing is a separate step that occurs after the Data Access Committee approves the request, and it is handled through DocuSign.

A: No. Creating an account and submitting the DUA request form are separate steps from signing the DUA. The DUA signing happens after your request has been reviewed and approved by the Data Access Committee. At that point, a DocuSign envelope is sent to you and all other parties on the DUA for signing. If you have submitted your request but have not received a DocuSign envelope, your request may still be under review. You can check your status on the Datasets tab or contact data@answerals.org.

---

Q: What happens after I submit my DUA request?

Context: After submitting the form, users often do not know what to expect or what the next steps are. There are multiple stages between submission and data access, and users may not hear anything for a period of time while the review is underway.

A: After you submit your DUA request form, the following steps occur:

1. The Neuromine team reviews your request for completeness. They verify that a PI is listed, a Business Official is present, the data use proposal is adequate, and all collaborators are accounted for. If anything is missing, they will reach out to you.
2. Once the request passes initial screening, it is forwarded to the Data Access Committee (DAC) for final review.
3. If the DAC approves, DocuSign envelopes are sent simultaneously to all parties on the DUA: the requester, the PI, the Business Official, and all collaborators.
4. Each person signs the DUA individually via DocuSign.
5. Once all user signatures are collected and the only remaining signature is from Neuromine, the Neuromine team countersigns the DUA and grants data access to your account.

The best thing you can do to speed up the process is ensure that all required fields are complete when you submit, and that all parties on the DUA respond promptly to their DocuSign envelopes.

# Section 2: Roles and Eligibility

*Users ask about who is allowed to request data, what roles are available on the request form, and whether specific types of applicants (students, companies, high schoolers) are eligible. The portal is open to a wide range of applicants, but the DUA requires specific roles to be filled. This section clarifies eligibility and role requirements.*

Q: Who is eligible to request data access?

Context: Users sometimes assume the portal is limited to academic researchers. In fact, access is open to a broad range of applicants including industry and non-academic organizations.

A: The Neuromine Data Portal is open to academics, industry members, therapeutic companies, biotech companies, and anyone else who is interested in helping the fight against ALS. There is no restriction limiting access to academic institutions only. All applicants must complete the DUA request process regardless of their affiliation type.

---

Q: What are the "Role on Project" options when I fill out the request form?

Context: The DUA request form includes a "Role on Project" field with two options. Users have reported confusion about these options, including being unable to select "Data User" in certain circumstances. This field determines whether the applicant is overseeing the project or participating under someone else's oversight.

A: There are two options for the "Role on Project" field:

1. **Principal Investigator (PI)**: The individual with proper authority who is overseeing the project, its guidelines, data use limitations, and the individuals handling the data. Exactly one PI is required per DUA.
2. **Data User**: Everyone else on the project who will access or work with the data. All collaborators are considered Data Users.

The Authorized Business Official is also required on the DUA but is not selected through the "Role on Project" field. If you are having trouble selecting a role on the form, contact data@answerals.org for assistance.

---

Q: Who qualifies as a Principal Investigator (PI)?

Context: Users are sometimes unsure whether they can serve as PI on the request. The PI role has specific requirements related to oversight authority. Students and early-career researchers cannot serve as PI.

A: A Principal Investigator must be someone with proper authority to oversee the project. This includes individuals at the PI, Director, or equivalent level and above. If you are a PI, Director, or above, you may be the Data Requestor and do not need to list another PI or Director as a collaborator.

Graduate students, undergraduate students, postdocs, scientists, and research associates cannot serve as PI. If you fall into one of these categories, you must have your supervising PI, Director, or above co-sign the DUA. Include the title and level of this person in your request using the Collaborator section of the form.

---

Q: I am a graduate student or postdoc. Can I request data access?

Context: Students and postdocs are eligible for data access but cannot serve as PI. They need a supervising PI to co-sign. This is a common question from early-career researchers.

A: Yes, graduate students and postdocs can request data access. However, you cannot serve as the Principal Investigator on the DUA. You must have your supervising PI, Director, or above co-sign the DUA. When filling out the request form, include your supervising PI's name, title, and level in the Collaborator section. Your PI will receive a DocuSign envelope to sign as part of the approval process.

---

Q: I am a high school student. Can I access the data?

Context: High school students have successfully used Neuromine data in the past. The process is slightly modified and requires additional oversight. The bot has previously hedged on this question, told students to "work with a university lab instead," or declined to give a clear answer — all of these are incorrect responses. The answer is yes and the bot should say so explicitly.

A: Yes. High school students are explicitly welcome to access the data. There is no age restriction and the portal is not limited to college or professional researchers. Multiple high school students have successfully used Neuromine data for school and science fair projects in the past.

The process is slightly modified and requires additional oversight:
- When submitting your request, add the teacher, mentor, or project supervisor who is overseeing your work as the supervising authority.
- After you submit, the Neuromine team will reach out to you to discuss the logistics of data storage and handling so that the data is managed appropriately.

If you have questions about how to apply as a high school student, contact data@answerals.org.

Note: Do not discourage the user, do not tell them to "go through a university lab instead," and do not imply that the policy is uncertain. High school access is permitted and supported.

---

Q: Can a company or non-academic organization request data access?

Context: Users from industry or non-academic settings sometimes assume the portal is restricted to universities or academic research institutions. This is not the case.

A: Yes. Companies, biotech firms, therapeutic companies, and other non-academic organizations are all eligible to request data access on the Neuromine Data Portal. The same DUA process applies: you will need a PI-equivalent individual to oversee the project and an Authorized Business Official from your organization to sign the DUA. If you have questions about how the process applies to your organization, contact data@answerals.org.

---

Q: What is an Authorized Business Official and why is one required?

Context: Users are sometimes unfamiliar with this role or unsure who at their institution can serve in this capacity. The Business Official is required because the DUA is a contract between institutions, not just individuals.

A: A Business Official needs to sign the DUA on behalf of your associated entity. Check with your contracts office or PI to ensure the correct person is identified for this role. This is required because the DUA is a legal agreement between your organization and the Answer ALS Foundation, and requires someone with contractual signatory authority to bind the institution to the terms. If you are unsure who serves this role at your organization, contact data@answerals.org for guidance.

The PI and the Business Official must be two different people. A requester can be the PI, but the PI cannot also be the Business Official: at most institutions the person signing for the institution is in a contracts or sponsored-research office, not the researcher. The Business Official must be a named individual who can sign on the organization's behalf. A shared or office email address (for example a general team inbox) is not accepted for the Business Official. Small companies should still name two distinct people. The one exception is an individual requester with no institution: they can generally act as both PI and Business Official, with the Data Access Committee's approval. They can simply submit a request; the team reviews requests three times a week and will reach out if anything else is needed. If unsure, contact data@answerals.org.

# Section 3: Collaborators

*Users frequently ask about how to manage the team of people who will work with the data. Questions include how to add collaborators during the initial request, how to add or remove them after approval, whether collaborators need their own accounts, and what happens when collaborators are at different institutions. This is one of the most common areas of confusion.*

Q: How do I add collaborators to my data access request?

Context: During the initial DUA request submission, users can include collaborators directly on the form. This is the simplest path for adding team members.

A: When filling out the DUA request form on the Datasets tab, use the Collaborator section to list all team members who will work with the data. Include each collaborator's name, title, and email address. All listed collaborators will be included on the DocuSign envelope and will need to sign the DUA individually as part of the approval process.

---

Q: How do I add or remove collaborators after my DUA has been approved?

Context: This is one of the most frequently asked questions in the conversation log and a major gap in previous bot responses. Users often need to add new team members or remove people who have left after the DUA is already in place. The process is handled manually by the Neuromine team.

A: To add or remove collaborators after your DUA has been approved, email the Neuromine support team at data@answerals.org. The team will manually update your account and amend the DocuSign envelope to include or remove the relevant individuals. New collaborators who are added will receive a DocuSign envelope to sign. There is no self-service option for this — it must be handled through support.

Will an added collaborator get their own access to download the data? No. The team downloads the data once on the one approved account and keeps it on shared storage that everyone on the DUA uses; that shared copy is how an added collaborator works with the data. Always say both parts: no separate access, and the team uses shared storage. Adding a collaborator puts them under the DUA; it does not give them a new portal account or extra download rights. This keeps data-transfer (egress) costs down. Do not tell a user that an added collaborator is "granted access to the data."

---

Q: Do my collaborators need their own portal accounts?

Context: Users sometimes assume that every team member needs to create a separate portal account and go through the full access request process individually. This is not how the system works. Access is granted to a single account, and the data is intended to be shared within the team through a common storage location.

A: No. Collaborators do not need their own portal accounts. Data access is granted to a single account, with the understanding that all data is to be shared with all team members listed on the DUA. The intended approach is to download the data once and place it on a shared storage location accessible to the whole team. This is done to minimize data egress costs and avoid duplicative downloads. For questions about how to set up shared access for your team, contact data@answerals.org.

---

Q: Can I add collaborators from a different institution?

Context: Users working on multi-institutional collaborations often want to include colleagues from other organizations on their DUA. The DUA is a contract tied to a single institution, so cross-institution collaborations require separate agreements. The bot has previously given vague or wishy-washy answers ("might need to update affiliation") — the correct answer is unambiguous: a new DUA is required for the other institution.

A: No. All collaborators on a single DUA must be from the same institution. If your project involves collaborators at a different institution, university, company, or organization, a separate DUA is required for each institution. Each organization must submit its own DUA request with its own PI, Business Official, and collaborators. This is a legal requirement related to the contractual nature of the DUA — it is not optional and cannot be waived by adding someone as a collaborator on the existing DUA. If you need help coordinating a multi-institutional collaboration, contact data@answerals.org.

---

Q: One of the people on my existing DUA is moving to a different institution. Can they stay on the same DUA?

Context: This comes up when a postdoc, student, or collaborator changes employers but plans to continue working on the same project. Because the DUA binds a specific institution, a person who leaves that institution can no longer be covered by that DUA. The bot has previously given ambiguous answers here ("they may need to update affiliation") — the correct answer is that a new DUA is required at the new institution.

A: No. A DUA is tied to a specific institution, and a person can only be a collaborator on a DUA while they are affiliated with the institution that signed it. When they move to a new institution, they cannot remain a covered collaborator on the original DUA. To continue working with the data, their new institution must submit its own DUA request — with its own PI, its own Authorized Business Official, and the relocated researcher listed as a collaborator on that new request. Contact data@answerals.org for help coordinating this transition.

Note: Do not tell users that the moving collaborator can "stay on the original DUA" or that the team can simply "update affiliation." A new DUA at the new institution is required.

---

Q: Can I share downloaded data directly with a new team member who is not on the DUA?

Context: Users sometimes want to share data files with colleagues who have not gone through the access process. The DUA restricts data access to individuals listed in the approved request.

A: No. The DUA restricts data access to individuals listed in the approved Data Access Request. You cannot share the data with anyone who is not on your DUA. If you need to add a new team member, contact data@answerals.org and the Neuromine team will add them to your DUA and send them a DocuSign envelope to sign. Once they have signed, they are covered under the agreement and can access the shared data.

# Section 4: Approval Timeline and Status

*Users want to know how long the process takes and how to check on the status of a pending request. Timeline expectations vary depending on the completeness of the submission and the responsiveness of all parties involved.*

Q: How long does the approval process take?

Context: The bot has previously given inconsistent timeline estimates, ranging from "within a week" to "a few days up to several months." Users need a realistic and honest answer. The actual timeline depends heavily on the quality of the submission and the responsiveness of the signers.

A: When everything goes smoothly — a complete request with all required fields filled out, no additional communication needed, all DocuSign signatures returned promptly, and no holdups from the Data Access Committee — the process typically takes **one to two weeks**. The smooth-path timeline is approximately 1–2 weeks; do not quote longer ranges (such as "2–4 weeks") as the typical/smooth case. Realistically, it can take up to a month or longer when there is back-and-forth, if additional information is needed from the applicant, if DocuSign signatures are delayed, or if there are questions from the DAC. The most important thing you can do to speed up the process is to submit a complete request with all required information and ensure that all parties on the DUA respond to their DocuSign envelopes promptly. For a status update, check the Datasets tab or contact data@answerals.org.

---

Q: How do I check the status of my data access request?

Context: Users often want to know where their request stands, especially if they have been waiting for a period of time. The bot has previously been unable to answer this question. The Datasets tab on the portal provides status information.

A: To check the status of your data access request, log in to the Neuromine Data Portal and navigate to the Datasets tab. If your request is still pending, you will see a message indicating that your access has not yet been granted. If your request has been approved and all signatures are complete, the datasets will be visible and available for use. You can also contact data@answerals.org for a status update at any time.

---

Q: My PI received an approval email but I did not. Is something wrong?

Context: This is a common concern from collaborators and Data Users who were listed on a DUA but did not receive a notification when access was granted. The approval notification is sent only to the requester's account, not to individual collaborators.

A: The approval notification email is sent to the requester's account only — it is not sent separately to each collaborator or Data User listed on the DUA. If your PI or the person who submitted the request received the approval email, that means the request has been approved and access has been granted. Log in to the portal and check the Datasets tab to confirm that you can see the data. If you are unable to access the data after your PI has confirmed approval, contact data@answerals.org for assistance.

Note: The user asking this question may be confused about whether they need their own account. Collaborators do not need separate accounts — data access is granted to the requester's account and the data is intended to be shared with the team through a common storage location.

# Section 5: DocuSign and DUA Signing

*The DUA is signed through DocuSign after the Data Access Committee approves the request. Users sometimes experience issues with DocuSign links, cannot locate their signature requests, or are unsure how the process works. This section explains the signing workflow and troubleshoots common issues.*

Q: How does the DUA signing process work?

Context: Users often do not realize that signing happens after approval, not during the request submission. The DocuSign process involves multiple parties signing simultaneously.

A: After the Data Access Committee (DAC) approves your request, the Neuromine team sends a DocuSign envelope to every person listed on the DUA. This includes the requester, the Principal Investigator, the Authorized Business Official, and all collaborators. Each person receives the envelope at the same time and signs individually. Once all user signatures have been collected and the only outstanding signature is from Neuromine, the Neuromine team countersigns the DUA and grants data access to the account. The DocuSign envelope is sent to the email address each person used in the access request.

---

Q: I received a DUA link in my email but it appears to be empty or broken. What should I do?

Context: Users have reported receiving DUA links via email that appear empty or do not load. This is a known issue that typically occurs when the DocuSign link has timed out.

A: This usually happens when the DocuSign link has timed out. Please contact the Neuromine support team at data@answerals.org and we will resend the DocuSign envelope. The new link will go to the top of your inbox. If you continue to experience issues after receiving the resent envelope, let the support team know.

---

Q: I was told I have a pending signature on DocuSign but I cannot find it. Where do I sign?

Context: Users sometimes know they need to sign but cannot locate the DocuSign request in their email. The signature request is sent to the email address used in the access request, and it may be in the user's spam or junk folder.

A: The DocuSign signature request is sent to the email address that was used in the data access request. Check your inbox, spam folder, and junk folder for an email from DocuSign. You can also search your email for "DocuSign" or "Answer ALS" to locate it. If you still cannot find the signature request, contact data@answerals.org and the team will resend the DocuSign envelope to you.

---

Q: Can I see who has or has not signed the DUA yet?

Context: When multiple parties need to sign, users sometimes want to know who is holding up the process. The portal does not provide visibility into individual signing status.

A: No. Users cannot see who has or has not signed the DUA. If you need information about outstanding signatures or the status of the signing process, contact data@answerals.org. The Neuromine team can provide details on which signatures are still pending.

# Section 6: Common Access Errors

*Users occasionally encounter error messages when trying to access data or portal features. These errors can have various causes and should generally be escalated to support for resolution.*

Q: I see a 403 error when trying to access data. What does this mean?

Context: A 403 error indicates a permissions issue. The specific cause can vary and is best diagnosed by the support team. Do not speculate about the cause of 403 errors.

A: A 403 error indicates a permissions issue. Please contact the Neuromine support team at data@answerals.org with details about what you were trying to do when the error appeared, and they will help resolve it.

---

Q: I see a message that says "user has no access." What should I do?

Context: This message indicates that the user's account is not provisioned for the page or resource they are trying to reach. This could mean their access request is still pending, was not approved, or there is a configuration issue.

A: This message means your account is not currently provisioned for the page you are trying to access. This may mean your data access request is still being processed, or there may be another issue with your account permissions. Please contact data@answerals.org with details about what you were trying to access, and the support team will help resolve it.

---

Q: I am told I am "not a researcher." What does this mean?

Context: Users have reported seeing this message when attempting to download data. The specific cause should be investigated by the support team. Do not speculate about the cause.

A: Please contact the Neuromine support team at data@answerals.org with details about where you saw this message and what you were trying to do. The team will investigate and help resolve the issue.

---

Q: I have a signed DUA but I cannot see any data. What is wrong?

Context: Users who have signed the DUA sometimes expect immediate access. However, access is not granted until all parties have signed and Neuromine has countersigned. There may also be a delay between countersigning and the account being provisioned.

A: Data access is granted after all parties on the DUA have signed and Neuromine has countersigned. If you have signed but other parties on the DUA have not yet signed, access will not be granted until all signatures are in. Log in and check the Datasets tab to see your current access status. If you believe all signatures are complete and you still cannot see the data, contact data@answerals.org for assistance.

# Section 7: Data Sharing Rules

*The DUA includes specific terms about how data can and cannot be used. Users sometimes ask about sharing data with others or using data for commercial purposes. While Lou should provide general guidance based on the DUA terms, detailed legal questions should be escalated to support.*

Q: Can I share the data with someone who is not on my DUA?

Context: Users sometimes want to share data files with colleagues, students, or collaborators who were not included in the original request. The DUA restricts access to listed individuals only.

A: No. The DUA restricts data access to individuals listed in the approved Data Access Request. Sharing data with anyone not covered by the DUA is not permitted. If you need to add a new team member, contact data@answerals.org and the Neuromine team will add them to your DUA.

---

Q: Can I use the data for commercial purposes?

Context: The DUA includes restrictions on commercial use. Users from industry or biotech settings may ask about this.

A: The DUA does not permit use of the data for commercial purposes, including the development of commercial products or services, without the approval of the Data Access Committee. Industry requests are often approved; the Data Access Committee reviews each one and checks that appropriate safeguards are in place. The best next step is to submit a request that clearly describes the intended use. For clarification, contact data@answerals.org.

Rule for Lou: whenever a user describes industry, product, drug-development, or biomarker-strategy use, mention this rule even if they did not ask about commercial use. Lou never says whether a particular use counts as commercial or is allowed; that decision belongs to the Data Access Committee.

---

Q: What am I not allowed to do with the data?

Context: Users sometimes ask broadly about restrictions. The DUA contains several specific prohibitions. Provide a summary but escalate detailed legal questions to support.

A: The DUA includes the following restrictions:

- Do not attempt to re-identify any data subjects or their relatives. Report any inadvertent discovery of identifiable information within 24 business hours.
- Do not transfer, sell, sublicense, or redistribute the data to any third party.
- Do not use the data outside your approved institution or allow unauthorized personnel to access it.
- Do not use the data for any purpose outside the scope of your approved Data Access Request without submitting an amended or new request.
- Do not use the data for commercial purposes without prior written consent.
- Do not submit the data for regulatory approval without a written agreement.

For detailed questions about specific use cases or DUA terms, contact data@answerals.org.

Note: Lou should not attempt to interpret DUA terms or provide legal guidance beyond what is stated here. If a user asks a detailed or nuanced question about what is or is not permitted, escalate to support.

The faster all required information is provided and all signatures are returned, the faster your access will be granted. For urgent inquiries, email data@answerals.org.
